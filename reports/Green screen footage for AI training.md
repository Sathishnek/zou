# Commercial matting models need footage you capture yourself

**No large, commercially usable source of green screen footage with alpha mattes exists off the shelf today.** The open academic matting datasets are research-only, unreleased, or of unclear license. Stock libraries, both free and paid, ban AI/ML training under their standard licenses. Training rights come only through separate, negotiated data-licensing programs. For research and prototyping, the de facto kit is VideoMatte240K (484 keyed green screen clips, about 240K frames) plus image-matting sets (Adobe DIM, Distinctions-646, P3M-10k, AM-2k) and background pools (BG-20k, DVM). This is the recipe behind RVM and MatAnyone, and it carries non-commercial obligations into any model trained on it. For a commercial product there are three clean routes. The first is to shoot your own green screen footage with AI-specific model releases and key it into foreground-plus-alpha labels. The second is to commission capture, or license a dataset, from a data vendor (Shutterstock data licensing, Storyblocks Data Licensing, Vecteezy Enterprise, Troveo, Wirestock). The third is to add 3D renders built from assets whose licenses allow ML training. None of the vendors checked here offered a ready-made green screen catalog, so custom capture briefs are the realistic path. Unverified license points are flagged throughout, because the research team could not read several stock sites and dataset pages directly.

## Every major alpha-matte dataset carries a research-only or unclear license

The public video matting datasets with real alpha were nearly all made the same way: researchers **bought green screen stock clips and keyed them in Adobe After Effects**. That origin explains why their licenses are so restrictive. **VideoMatte240K** (BGMv2, CVPR 2021) contains 484 green screen videos, 384 in 4K and 100 in HD, yielding **240,709 frames** of alpha and foreground ([CVPR paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Lin_Real-Time_High-Resolution_Background_Matting_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)). It is distributed as a 6 GB JPEG SD set and a 60 GB JPEG HD set ([RVM training.md](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/documentation/training.md)). The BGMv2 code is MIT-licensed ([BGMv2 GitHub](https://github.com/PeterL1n/BackgroundMattingV2)). The data's own license, however, could not be confirmed, and a GitHub issue questions it ([issue #208](https://github.com/PeterL1n/BackgroundMattingV2/issues/208)). Its sibling PhotoMatte13K (13,665 studio green screen photos) was cut to **just 85 released mattes because of "privacy and licensing issues"** ([BGMv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.07810)).

MatAnyone's larger **VM800** (826 green screen clips bought from Storyblocks, Envato Elements and Motion Array) "cannot be publicly released" for licensing reasons ([MatAnyone TRAIN.md](https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone/blob/main/doc/TRAIN.md)). The field's newest and largest set is **VMReal** (MatAnyone 2, CVPR 2026: 28K real clips, 2.4M frames). It uses no green screen at all: mattes are auto-labelled and filtered by a learned quality evaluator. VMReal is **still listed as a TODO, not released** ([MatAnyone2 GitHub](https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone2)).

Two structural facts matter more than any single row. First, **license contamination propagates through the standard training recipe**. RVM's final stage trains on "ImageMatte," which is Distinctions-646 plus Adobe DIM, both non-commercial ([RVM training.md](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/documentation/training.md)). Adobe's agreement states that models pretrained on DIM "can only be used and distributed for noncommercial purposes" ([GCA-Matting README](https://github.com/Yaoyi-Li/GCA-Matting/blob/master/README.md)). Second, the "MIT" tag on the JizhiziLi datasets (P3M, AM-2k, AIM-500, BG-20k) covers the annotations. The images themselves were collected from the web under "free-use licenses," and the P3M release agreement frames the data as "aimed to aid research efforts" ([P3M agreement](https://jizhizili.github.io/files/p3m_dataset_agreement/P3M-10k_Dataset_Release_Agreement.pdf)). Use these as a research baseline and a public benchmark. Do not treat them as a commercially cleared foundation.

The auxiliary sets that go with the matting data are also standard. They are COCO, YouTubeVIS 2021 and Supervisely Person for segmentation co-training, and the DVM background videos (14.6 GB train) as compositing backgrounds ([RVM training.md](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/documentation/training.md)). Hugging Face and Kaggle have thin coverage; the research team could not browse them fully. Papers with Code keeps dataset pages for [VideoMatte240K](https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/videomatte240k) and [P3M-10k](https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/p3m-10k). The [MSU Video Matting Benchmark](https://videomatting.com/) is useful for evaluation.

## Stock libraries sell green screen clips but forbid training on them

The academic habit of 2021, buying green screen clips on Envato or Storyblocks and keying them, is now explicitly prohibited on most platforms. **Every major free stock video site bans ML training by default.** Pexels forbids using its API "to collect Pexels photos/videos or metadata at scale to train, fine-tune, evaluate, or otherwise develop ML/AI models or datasets, unless you have explicit permission" ([Pexels AI and ML FAQ](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/27292485713945-AI-and-ML-FAQ)). Yet Pexels reserves the right for itself and "third party partners specifically authorised by Pexels" to use contributor content for machine learning. That asymmetry signals a B2B data channel through its owner, Canva. ProductionCrate goes further: accepting its terms is framed as an **"express contractual waiver" of any fair-use defense** for AI use ([ProductionCrate Terms](https://www.productioncrate.com/terms.html)).

The best-matched buys are Shutterstock and Storyblocks. Both already hold green screen stock of the kind used to build VideoMatte240K and VM800, and both now sell training rights to it. ## Synthetic data complements real green screen footage but cannot replace it

The most proven "synthetic" technique is **compositing** rather than rendering. Real foregrounds with keyed alpha are composited onto large background pools, with continuous temporal augmentation (affine, colour jitter, noise, blur) applied to both layers. RVM combined 484 foreground clips with **3,118 human-free background videos and 8,000 background images** ([RVM paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.11515)). Composites have a known failure: MatAnyone 2 criticises earlier sets as "purely synthetic composites" ([MatAnyone 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11782v1)). For that reason researchers co-train on real segmentation data and, increasingly, on auto-labelled real footage.

Generative video is the most tempting shortcut and the weakest option. The clips still need keying. They carry generator artifacts in exactly the hair and edge regions the model must learn. Veo output also carries an invisible SynthID watermark ([InVideo](https://invideo.io/blog/kling-3-vs-veo-3-1/)). And the terms of the main providers either forbid building competing models or hand training rights back to the provider. MAGICK showed the idea works for still images: generate subjects on a key colour, then key them ([MAGICK](https://github.com/RyannDaGreat/MAGICK)). But MAGICK itself was released non-commercially.

## Clean labels depend on capture quality, despill and a clean plate

If you shoot your own footage, label quality is decided on set. **Record 4:2:2 or 4:4:4 at 10-bit or RAW, as lightly compressed as possible** (ProRes 422 HQ/4444 or RAW). 4:2:0 subsampling causes jagged edges and colour bleeding in the key ([Luminxel](https://luminxel.com/chroma-subsampling-explained-444-422-420/); [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/chroma-key-green-screen-guide/)). Light the screen evenly and separately from the subject. Keep the subject about 6 feet from the screen to limit spill and shadows ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/ng/creativecloud/video/discover/how-to-use-green-screen.html)). Shoot at 4K: RVM's high-resolution stages train on 2048p crops, and VideoMatte240K is mostly 4K ([RVM training.md](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/documentation/training.md)). Beyond the cited sources, the research team offers some practical recommendations. Keep natural motion blur so the model learns fractional alpha. Vary hairstyles, since hair is where matting metrics diverge. Avoid green wardrobe except as deliberate hard cases. Capture a clean plate whenever the lighting or camera changes.

Pulling the matte is harder than it looks, because **single-background keying is mathematically under-constrained**. The only exact real-capture method is Smith and Blinn's **triangulation matting**: filming a static subject against two known backgrounds lets you solve for both alpha and foreground colour ([Smith & Blinn 1996](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs148-09-fall/papers/smith-blinn.pdf)). This makes it ideal for a small gold-standard evaluation set. For production volume, use a professional keyer (After Effects Keylight with Key Cleaner and Advanced Spill Suppressor is the research norm ([survey snippet](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07905v1))) and a clean-plate difference. Use ffmpeg `chromakey` and `despill` only for previews or rough masks. Setting `blend=0` produces a **binary** alpha, and the YUV-distance model approximates edges ([FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html); [despill docs](https://ayosec.github.io/ffmpeg-filters-docs/8.0/Filters/Video/despill.html)).

Keying and despill are separate outputs, and a training set needs both. Without despill, green fringes baked into the foreground colour reappear in every composite, and the model learns them. The emerging QA step is to cross-check each keyed matte against an AI matting model. MatAnyone 2's Matting Quality Evaluator produces a per-pixel error map without ground truth. MatAnyone 2 reports roughly 26% lower MAD than MatAnyone 1 on CRGNN; the published numbers come via a secondary summary ([MatAnyone 2](https://pq-yang.github.io/projects/MatAnyone2/)). Frames where the keyer and the model disagree are the ones to send for manual review.

| Stage | Recommended spec |
|---|---|
| Camera / codec | 4K; 10-bit or RAW; 4:2:2/4:4:4; ProRes 422 HQ, 4444 or RAW ([Luminxel](https://luminxel.com/chroma-subsampling-explained-444-422-420/)) |
| Set | Evenly lit screen, lit separately; subject about 6 ft from the screen ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/ng/creativecloud/video/discover/how-to-use-green-screen.html)); clean plate for each setup |
| Matte extraction | Pro keyer plus clean plate; despill as a separate pass; triangulation for the gold eval set ([Smith & Blinn](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs148-09-fall/papers/smith-blinn.pdf)) |
| Layout | `clip/fgr/` (despilled foreground) + `clip/pha/` (alpha), as in VideoMatte240K and RVM ([RVM](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/documentation/training.md)); optional `bgr/` clean plate and derived `trimap/` |
| Precision | Alpha as 16-bit PNG or EXR; record whether alpha is straight or premultiplied, since a mismatch causes fringes ([Blender/AE EXR issue](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/exr-multilayer-alpha-channel-issues-from-blender-50978)) |
| Scale target | A few hundred diverse clips can fine-tune an existing model: RVM's main matting stages used 484 ([RVM paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.11515)). This is an inference; no minimum-size study was found |

## Consent for filmed people is the licensing risk teams overlook

Clearing the footage does not clear the people in it. Standard stock model releases were written for advertising and editorial use. Getty/iStock and Canva (owner of Pexels and Pixabay) explicitly exclude uses aimed at "identification of natural persons" ([iStock](https://www.istockphoto.com/legal/license-agreement); [Canva](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/)). Illinois' BIPA allows **$1,000–$5,000 per violation, and each facial scan can count separately**. Courts have held that publicly posted images are not consent ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/new-biometrics-lawsuits-signal-potential-legal-risks-ai)). GDPR treats biometric data as a special category that requires a lawful basis and a DPIA ([Surveillant.ai](https://surveillant.ai/guides/ai-video-surveillance-privacy-laws)). A matting model that never extracts face templates may fall outside the definition of a "biometric identifier," but no case law tests that. The prudent course is releases that name AI training explicitly, which is the model Adobe and Wirestock already use.

The other pitfalls are contractual. Keyed mattes are derivatives of the source footage, which is why VM800 could not be released. Weights trained on Adobe DIM stay non-commercial wherever they end up. Envato's policy covers "indirect" training. Scraping remains legally live: *Getty v. Stability AI* was decided in the UK High Court in November 2025 ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/getty-images-v-stability-ai-what-the-high-courts-decision-means-for-rights-holders-and-ai-developers)). Several licenses here were read from search excerpts, not full pages. Confirm the exact current wording before relying on any of them legally.

## Recommended action plan

The plan runs in two tracks: benchmark with academic data now, while building a commercially clean corpus in parallel.

| Phase | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Baseline (weeks 1–2) | Download VideoMatte240K, P3M-10k, AM-2k, BG-20k and the DVM backgrounds. Reproduce RVM or MatAnyone and benchmark on VideoMatte240K-Test, YouTubeMatte and CRGNN | Research baseline; **keep weights internal, non-commercial** |
| 2. Legal scoping (parallel) | Get written terms for VideoMatte240K data and the JizhiziLi release agreements. Draft an AI-training model release. Settle BIPA/GDPR posture | Cleared-source policy |
| 3. Buy/commission (weeks 2–8) | Request quotes from Shutterstock and Storyblocks data licensing (existing green screen stock), Vecteezy Enterprise, and Wirestock or Troveo (custom green screen capture brief using the spec table) | Licensed raw plates at roughly $1–7 per minute |
| 4. In-house capture | Run 4K 10-bit 4:2:2+ shoots with clean plates, diverse hair, wardrobe and motion. Shoot a small static triangulation set for gold eval | Owned plates plus exact-alpha test set |
| 5. Label pipeline | Pro keyer → separate despill → `fgr`/`pha` at 16-bit → AI cross-check (MatAnyone 2/RVM) → manual review of disagreement frames | Commercial-clean foreground/alpha corpus |
| 6. Augment | Composite onto owned or licensed backgrounds with temporal and harmonization augmentation. Add Blender renders from ML-permitted assets for hair and motion blur. Skip MetaHuman and commercial generator output | Scaled training set |
| 7. Retrain | Train from scratch or from a commercially licensed checkpoint on clean data only. Compare against the Phase 1 baseline | Shippable model |

## Conclusion

The field's own trajectory proves the licensing problem. The researchers who built VideoMatte240K and VM800 bought green screen stock clips and could release only part of their data, or none at all. The newest state of the art, VMReal, dropped green screen for auto-labelled real footage because clean, redistributable keyed data does not scale. For a company, the cleanest dataset is one whose rights you created: a few hundred well-shot, properly released green screen clips, expanded by compositing. That is the scale RVM worked at, and within reach for one production team. Stock and vendor channels are best used for diversity at the margins, not as the foundation.

Two developments are worth watching, because either would change this calculus. One is a public release of VMReal, or of its quality-evaluator pipeline, under permissive terms. That would let teams pseudo-label ordinary licensed footage without any green screen. The other is a data vendor launching a keyed, alpha-labelled green screen product. Until either happens, owning the capture and the keying pipeline is the durable advantage, not access to a dataset.
